Keeping Your Laurel Water Heater Running Longer

Many early failures trace back to maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. If you have a tankless unit, descale it yearly where the water is hard so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Common Water Heater Problems in Laurel

Water heaters in and around Laurel deal with a familiar set of stresses. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, hard water may be a real factor: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the popping and rumbling you may hear when the heater fires. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, and that's why regular descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

We also see a lot of aging equipment. A tank-style water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod has often been eaten away, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and heating elements and thermostats on electric ones.

Code requirements add another layer. Replacing an older heater frequently means bringing the install up to current code: an expansion tank if the water system is closed, a relief valve drain line piped to an approved spot, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, and a drain pan or seismic straps where required. Homes on well water have their own issues, such as heavier sediment, iron staining, and the sulfur odor that sometimes develops when certain water chemistry reacts with a standard anode rod. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most cost far less when caught early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a quick diagnostic visit can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor. Give us a call at (443) 405-4339 to set up a visit.
