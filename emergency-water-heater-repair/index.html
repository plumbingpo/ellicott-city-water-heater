How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • The smell of gas near the unit, a situation that calls for evacuating and phoning the utility or 911
  • An electric water heater that keeps tripping its circuit breaker
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly
  • Standing water around the water heater that returns after you wipe it up
  • A T&P valve that releases water repeatedly instead of staying closed
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • Black soot or heat discoloration around the burner door or draft hood
  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house

Why Hire a Professional for Emergency Water Heater Repair?

A water heater emergency packs several hazards into one closet or garage corner. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas models. There is a tank holding very hot water under pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, is doing so for a reason. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, crank on a leaking fitting until it snaps, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into something far worse. The safest moves for a homeowner are the ones described above: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

Professional help is not just about having wrenches. It is about knowing why the failure happened so it does not repeat a few weeks later. When a relief valve discharges, we measure static water pressure and look for a missing or failed expansion tank. When a tank floods the floor, we locate the true source, whether it is the valve, a fitting, a connector, or the tank wall. When a gas heater dies, we check the thermocouple or flame sensor, gas pressure, and venting before restarting it. The diagnosis determines everything that follows. Components can be replaced the same visit; a failed tank cannot be patched, and we say so plainly and explain the replacement path.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our recommendations:

  • Lift the relief valve lever once a year to confirm it opens and reseats, and call if it keeps dripping
  • Find and label the cold-water shutoff at the heater and the main house valve now, before you ever need them
  • Set the temperature around 120F, which protects against burns and slows tank wear
  • Consider an automatic leak shutoff that stops the cold supply the moment water hits the floor
  • Flush sediment from the tank yearly, since buildup overheats the bottom and speeds corrosion
  • Keep boxes, paint, gasoline, and cleaning products well away from a gas water heater
  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
